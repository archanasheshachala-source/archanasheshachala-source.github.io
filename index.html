From molecule to device

Light creates mobile electrons and positive charge carriers called holes in the perovskite. I study the organic layer that extracts holes, and its contact with the perovskite.

Can a transport molecule also interact with surface defects?

Pyrazine-derived materials · First author · ChemPlusChem, 2025

Imperfections at the perovskite surface can cause useful charge to be lost. I studied pyrazine-based molecules whose nitrogen atoms can interact with surface lead sites while the material acts as a hole-transport layer.

My contribution: Design and synthesis of pyrazine-derived materials, with experimental and theoretical analysis of their interfacial behaviour.

What the evidence shows

Interaction experiments, spectroscopy, and modelling supported nitrogen–lead interaction and a role in charge transfer. The devices showed moderate efficiency.

MaterialPCE

Methods & technical detail

Dopant-free pyrazine-derived hole-transport materials were examined as charge-extracting layers and potential interfacial passivating components. Interaction studies with PbI₂, photophysical/device analysis, and theoretical modelling probed coordination at undercoordinated Pb²⁺ sites and its relationship to charge transfer and recombination.

Organic synthesis · PbI₂ interaction studies · Photophysics · Theoretical modelling
